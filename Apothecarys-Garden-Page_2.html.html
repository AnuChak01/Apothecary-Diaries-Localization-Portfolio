The Apothecary's Garden

「人は薬と毒の区別を知らずに生きている。」 "People live without knowing the difference between medicine and poison."

In 薬屋のひとりごと, flowers and herbs are never merely decoration. They are characters in their own right — each one carrying a history, a warning, or a meaning woven deliberately into the story's fabric.

She understood what danger lies beneath beauty, what silence truly meant, and what others so easily missed, be it herb, flower, people, or the situations that tested her resilience, knowledge and intelligence. This is Maomao's Compendium. From her perspective. From the world she faced.

Every card has two faces, the truth lies on the other side 表裏二面、真実は裏に眠る
片喰
Katabami · カタバミ
Wood Sorrel

「ただの片喰（カタバミ）ですよ。よくクローバーと間違えられますが、葉の形をよく見てください。綺麗なハート型でしょう。」

"It's just Wood Sorrel. Most people mistake it for common clover — but look closely at the leaves. They are perfect hearts."

「それに、とても繊細な植物です。日が沈んだり雨が降ったりすると、身を縮めるように葉を閉じます。これを『就眠運動』と言います。条件が良くないときは、自分の本当の姿を隠すんです。」

"It is also a very sensitive plant. When the sun goes down or the rain starts to fall, it folds its leaves inward, as if shrinking away from the world. It's called 'sleeping.' It hides its true shape when the conditions aren't right."

（後宮の多くのものと同じだ。表向きは愛らしく無害に見えても、その内側には腐食を削り取るほどの酸を秘めている。あるいは、不用意に触れれば腹を下す毒にもなる。）

Like many things in the Rear Palace — it looks harmless and sweet on the surface. But underneath, it's acidic enough to strip away corrosion, or cause a stomach ache if you're not careful.

喰
花言葉  ·  Significance in the Series

Maomao's flower is 片喰（かたばみ）— Wood Sorrel. Her name, 猫猫, literally means "cat cat" — and in Japanese folk tradition, Wood Sorrel carries an old association with cats. The parallel is deliberate. Wood sorrel is a wild plant — small, overlooked, spreads quietly into every corner it isn't meant to occupy. It closes its leaves in unfavorable conditions, hiding its true shape until the conditions are right again. Same as Maomao.

桔梗
Kikyo · キキョウ
Kikyo

「それは桔梗（キキョウ）です。見た目は可憐ですが、その本質は根にあります。桔梗の根は、去痰や鎮咳の薬として古くから重宝されてきました。今の貴妃様に最も必要な薬の一つです。」

"That is Kikyo. While it looks lovely, its true value lies in the roots — long prized as an expectorant and cough suppressant. It is one of the medicines you need most right now, My Lady."

「蕾を見てください。星形に開く前、風船のようにパンパンに膨らんでいます。まるで、溢れ出しそうな想いを必死に閉じ込めているようです。」

"Look at the buds. Before they open into stars, they swell up tight like balloons — as if desperately holding in emotions that are about to overflow."

（花言葉は『変わらぬ愛』。そして『誠実』。子供を失った悲しみを誰にも見せず、ただ独りで耐え忍ぶ。この花は、凛とした貴妃様そのものだ。）

The flower's language is "Unchanging Love" and "Honesty." To endure the grief of losing a child alone, showing it to no one. This flower is the image of the dignified Consort herself.

桔
花言葉  ·  Significance in the Series

Maomao's first case in the Rear Palace — a mysterious illness stealing the lives of newborns. The poison is lead-based cosmetics, and the antidote is the root of the flower, Kikyo. A flower that heals what beauty poisoned.

In Japan, Kikyo is one of the seven flowers of autumn (秋の七草) — a symbol of longing, fidelity, and restrained emotion. Kikyo is also the name of a color used in the Heian period for the clothing of the nobility.

In Chinese medicine, 桔梗根 (Jié gěng) is a real herb prized for treating lung ailments and throat conditions.

"There are hundreds, thousands of flowers in this world, but I don't think one can decide whether a Peony or a Bellflower is more beautiful."

Consort Gyokuyou is truly like a peony. But the more beautiful the flower, the more likely it is to be poisonous. There is a reason she continues to bloom in this garden called the Inner Court without withering.

牡
花言葉  ·  Significance in the Series

In China, the Peony is called The King of Flowers — a symbol of wealth, honour and imperial splendour. A woman compared to a peony is the most commanding presence in the room.

In Japan, the Peony symbolises bravery and honour — the mark of a noble who does not shrink.

Consort Gyokuyou of the Jade Pavilion is the peony of the Rear Palace — full-bloomed, commanding, impossible to overlook. In Maomao's eyes, the more beautiful the flower, the more likely it is to be poisonous. To survive and bloom without withering in the Inner Court takes more than grace — and Gyokuyou has it all.

毒
薬知識  ·  Apothecary Knowledge

In the Northern forest of the Rear Palace, Jinshi tasks Maomao with an investigation — where she discovers the vibrant, lethal Fire Coral Mushroom, only to learn it had bloomed atop a shallow grave of the late Lady Jin. While others recoil in horror, Maomao blushes with delight at the rare specimen. She calls it "corpse-fed" — these fungi thrive on the nutrients of decaying organic matter, confirming her deduction that a body lies buried beneath.

O-O-OX Bezoar!!

黄
薬知識  ·  Apothecary Knowledge

牛黄 (ごおう · Go-ou) is a calcified stone found in the gallbladder of oxen, considered extraordinarily precious. In Chinese medicine, it stands among the highest tier of medicinal substances.

Maomao's internal monologue specifically uses the phrase 「馬には人参、猫には牛黄」which literally translates to "Carrots for horses, ox bezoar for cats." A metaphor Maomao uses, meaning giving each thing or person exactly what they desire or need most. Jinshi, knowing this well, explicitly used the bezoar as bait to convince Maomao to investigate dangerous or complex mysteries within the palace.
